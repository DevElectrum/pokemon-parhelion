# Pokémon Parhelion — canon and working design

**Last updated:** 2026-11-05. Creative director decisions are confirmed; assistant-detailed mechanics are marked **working**, not separately approved item-by-item. This is a compact in-repository canon reference, not a complete script.

## Confirmed world

- Two hostile regions: a **Gen 8-aligned, more martial/industrial** region where the story starts, and a **Gen 9-aligned, more academic/research-focused** region encountered substantially at **Rank III**. Neither region is uniformly good or evil; each contains internal divisions.
- The world map broadly evokes a **Poké Ball**. The dangerous middle circle is **No Man's Land**, riddled with Ultra Wormholes. Exact local geography and names are **open**.
- A devastating war **centuries ago** abused six chosen legendaries. The Gen 8 side exploited **Zacian, Zamazenta, Tapu Koko**; the Gen 9 side exploited **Koraidon, Miraidon, Tapu Lele**. **Eternatus is not among the six.**
- Human powers awoke **Necrozma**, provoking **Solgaleo and Lunala** and a massive Ultra Wormhole catastrophe. Responsibility and the original reason for the war are not settled in the script. Civilians usually blame the opposing region.
- The war never reached lasting peace: a centuries-long **temporary truce/armistice** followed the catastrophe. Military and economic activity is increasing again.
- Some Ultra Beasts coexist with people; others are dangerous. Ultra Beasts are beginning to **Terastallize**, making hostile encounters harder to contain.
- The **Terastal Revolution** is a modern industrial and scientific competition to harvest/use Terastal energy. It provides potential benefits but also fuels strategic rivalry, particularly around No Man's Land. **Do not assert** that energy extraction causes wormholes, UB changes or Terapagos's revival without a later story decision.
- **Calyrex's prophecy** foretells the return of **Terapagos**, believed dead, alongside a chosen figure. Terapagos is **not** an opening starter and appears mysteriously later.

## Confirmed roster and protagonist

- Only Pokémon **introduced** in generations **7, 8 or 9** are intended for this setting. Older species appearing in later games are not automatically eligible. Edge cases such as new evolutions of earlier species require a separate decision.
- Gen 8-origin species cluster in the martial region; Gen 9-origin species cluster in the academic region; ordinary Gen 7 Pokémon are distributed. Ultra Beast placement is special.
- The **19-year-old protagonist** initially dislikes Pokémon, but bonds with a wounded captive escaped from traffickers.
- The pre-game choice determines which wounded companion appears in the shared opening: **Type: Null, Jangmo-o, Dreepy, or Frigibax**. A possible later **Poipole** alternative is **deferred**; Terapagos is never one of the initial four.
- The protagonist's parents possibly being killed in an Ultra Beast incident was **floated, not confirmed**.

## Confirmed opening conflict

- A **criminal organization based in the Gen 8 starting region** captures/transports powerful Pokémon toward a **clandestine Gen 9 laboratory**. Do **not** invent the lab's experiment, sponsor, exact affiliation or faction leadership.
- During a **thunderstorm**, the chosen wounded starter escapes a small crew's shipment. The protagonist finds it and nurses it for **weeks**.
- Low-level couriers trace it to the protagonist's home and demand it back. The protagonist's **first ordinary Pokémon battle** follows. This is explicitly **not** an official duel.

## Confirmed official duel system (details below working)

- A six-tier **official / nobility-inspired hierarchy** replaces the ordinary eight-gym route. Distinct, equivalent rank names exist across regions; a title confers defined official standing/office, not sovereign rule.
- Ordinary battles and official duels are different. Formal duels between the **same two people** occur no more than **once per in-world year**; ordinary battles have no such restriction.
- Peer-qualification duels against **distinct same-rank rivals** grant eligibility to challenge a higher official. A successful **claim duel** transfers the specified office and attached authority. Duties can be delegated; this is not a political management game.
- The protagonist canonically never loses **formal** duels: player losses are rewound/retried and do not become story events. **This does not automatically apply to the opening ordinary battle.**

**Assistant-designed working tier names:** Gen 8: Knight → Banneret → Castellan → Warden → Marshal → High Marshal. Gen 9: Fellow → Assessor → Arbiter → Prefect → Provost → Chancellor. **Working qualifying-win thresholds:** 8, 6, 4, 2, 1, then one claim duel per promotion (five claims). User directed **descending** qualification counts because top ranks have fewer peers; numbers remain tunable.

## Production and visual rules

- Main cast **fully custom**; recurring supports **generator base + hand customization**; generic crowds, random trainers and low-rank grunts **seeded procedural**.
- Black/White-inspired taller, expressive original character art adapted to GBA constraints.
- The NPC Sprite Lab v0.1 is only an early procedural **overworld sprite** experiment, not polished production art and not a generator of matching trainer battle portraits.
- Design and implement the story **incrementally**, beginning only with the prologue through first battle. No complete novel-length scenario is required in advance.

## Not yet canon

Region/town names; exact geography; protagonist's custom design; parent history; first grunt's party; starter selection screen details; first ordinary-battle loss handling; criminal/lab names, experiment and leadership; ancient war cause; Terapagos's exact fate; the ending.
